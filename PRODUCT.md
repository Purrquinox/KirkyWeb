# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Interest-based niche communities: developers, designers, musicians, writers, and other makers who want a less noisy, more intentional alternative to mainstream social platforms. They come for the aesthetic, stay for the community. They're discerning — they'll notice lazy design immediately. They use the app daily, on desktop during work and mobile on the go.

## Product Purpose

Kirky is a creator-focused social platform. The web application gives niche communities a place to share posts, follow people they care about, explore trending topics, and receive notifications — without the chaos of mainstream social. Success means a user opens Kirky instead of the default because it feels sharper and more deliberate.

## Brand Personality

Bold, editorial, precise. The platform has a point of view and isn't afraid to show it. It feels designed by someone with taste, not assembled from a component library.

## Anti-references

See design.md for the visual language that governs what this should NOT look like. Key principle: no decoration for decoration's sake, no component-kit aesthetic, no generic dark mode, no crypto/Web3 neon-on-black. The design should feel inevitable, not assembled.

## Design Principles

1. **One system, two viewports** — the mobile app and web app should feel like siblings, not strangers. Same color system, same typography weight conventions, same motion character.
2. **Hierarchy through weight, not chrome** — every surface should be legible without borders, shadows, or cards doing the heavy lifting.
3. **Earned interactivity** — every hover, transition, and active state should feel considered. No gratuitous animation.
4. **Floating, not anchored** — navigation and compose affordances should feel light. The interface never feels heavy.
5. **Context-aware density** — desktop gets more columns and density; mobile gets the focused single-column experience from the native app.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum. Respect prefers-reduced-motion. Ensure color is never the sole carrier of meaning (pair with icons and text). Focus states must be visibly distinct, not just the browser default ring.
